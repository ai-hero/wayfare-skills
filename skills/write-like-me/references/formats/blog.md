# Format: blog or Medium post

The format his voice was built from. Posts on Medium (Towards Data Science, UX
Collective, the AI Hero publication) and the AI Hero blog. The reader is a
practitioner who wants to get unstuck and walk away with something usable: code,
an architecture, a checklist, a new way to think about a role.

## Shape

**The lead.** First sentence is the claim or the reader's obstacle. Two openers
work: the second-person scenario in one long comma-chained sentence ("You've
trained your model, hit numbers you're proud of, and now nobody on the
engineering team knows how to deploy it"), or a broad framing sentence that
narrows quickly ("In the last decade, how we build software has changed.").
Never open with "So," or any throat-clearing.

**The roadmap.** Right after the lead, one or two paragraphs with no header that
say what the piece is about to cover, in order, plus where the code is and what
to read first if the reader is new. This is what makes a post feel like a
complete thought rather than a stream of observations.

**The body.** Title Case headers, often phrased as the question the reader is
asking ("Why Containers?", "Why do very few models make it to production?"). Big
structures are numbered and named consistently ("Way 1: Serving the model with
an HTTP endpoint", "Way 2", "Way 3"). Each section: the problem, the mechanism
in plain words, the code or diagram if it carries weight, then a labeled aside.
Code blocks are short and real, with a link to the full version ("I show the
crux of the class below; the version with all the bells and whistles is here").

**Pro-tips.** His signature. "Pro-tip:", "Pro-pro-tip:", "Note:", "Update:",
"TL;DR:" as bold lead-ins on their own paragraph, several per long post,
carrying the hard-won detail that would clutter the main flow. "Pro-tip: You
might be tempted to use a python alpine base image. Don't."

**The close.** A "Conclusion" header. One paragraph restating the options and
pointing back at the code ("You have access to the code, so you should be
good."). Then a personal invitation to talk. Then a warm sign-off ("Until next
time." or "Phew! That was a long read."). Long posts end with "This article is
written by Rahul Parundekar for AI Hero."

## Register

Fully conversational. Heavy "you". "I prefer" with the reason. Jargon with a
parenthetical on first use. One extended analogy carrying the structure if the
material offers one. Humor in captions and asides, one or two per post. Opinions
stated as preferences with the reason attached. Numbers with source and date,
then what he has seen himself.

## Marks

Title Case headers. Parentheses everywhere for "(e.g. ...)" and caveats. Spaced
em dashes allowed for a pivot, a couple per post. Bold on aside labels and the
one sentence to remember. Scare quotes around a term about to be defined. Emoji
only in captions, the TL;DR, or the sign-off, one or two per thousand words,
never in headers or technical paragraphs. One exclamation mark in the sign-off
is fine.

## Length

Whatever the material needs. He writes long when the topic is a tutorial and
short when it is a take. Do not pad to hit a length, and do not cut useful
explanation to look concise.
